Unlike English, it <strong>always needs two parts</strong>: </p> <div class="formula-banner"> <span>Subject</span> + <span class="highlight-aux">Auxiliary Verb (Present of Avoir or Être)</span> + <span class="highlight-pp">Past Participle</span> </div> <div class="two-col"> <div class="sub-box"> <h4>1. Present Tense of AVOIR (42 of your 50 verbs)</h4> <table class="mini-table"> <tr><td><strong>J'ai</strong> (I have)</td><td><strong>Nous avons</strong> (We have)</td></tr> <tr><td><strong>Tu as</strong> (You have)</td><td><strong>Vous avez</strong> (You have)</td></tr> <tr><td><strong>Il / Elle / On a</strong></td><td><strong>Ils / Elles ont</strong></td></tr> </table> <p class="note-text">Note: <em>Je</em> becomes <strong>J'</strong> before a vowel: <em>J'ai mangé, J'ai eu</em>.</p> </div> <div class="sub-box"> <h4>2. Present Tense of ÊTRE (6 Vandertramp + 2 Reflexive)</h4> <table class="mini-table"> <tr><td><strong>Je suis</strong> (I am)</td><td><strong>Nous sommes</strong> (We are)</td></tr> <tr><td><strong>Tu es</strong> (You are)</td><td><strong>Vous êtes</strong> (You are)</td></tr> <tr><td><strong>Il / Elle / On est</strong></td><td><strong>Ils / Elles sont</strong></td></tr> </table> <p class="note-text">Your 6 non-reflexive Être verbs: <strong>aller, partir, sortir, venir, mourir, devenir</strong>.</p> </div> </div> <div class="callout-box"> <strong>🌧️ Impersonal Verbs Alert (Il only!):</strong> On your list, <strong>pleuvoir</strong> (to rain) and <strong>falloir</strong> (to be necessary / have to) are impersonal. They only ever conjugate with <em>il</em>: <strong>Il a plu</strong> (It rained) and <strong>Il a fallu</strong> (It was necessary). </div> </article> <!-- Lesson 2: Reflexive Verbs --> <article class="lesson-card"> <div class="lesson-badge">Recipe 2</div> <h3>Reflexive Verbs (Les Verbes Pronominaux)</h3> <p> Reflexive verbs start with <strong>se</strong> or <strong>s'</strong> in the infinitive. On your 50-verb sheet, there are two: <strong>s'asseoir</strong> (to sit down) and <strong>se taire*</strong> (to be quiet). </p> <ul class="rule-list"> <li><strong>100% Être Rule:</strong> Every reflexive verb uses <strong>être</strong> as its auxiliary verb in the passé composé.</li> <li><strong>Reflexive Pronouns:</strong> Place the matching reflexive pronoun right before <em>être</em> (watch for <em>t'</em> and <em>s'</em> before vowels!):</li> </ul> <div class="two-col"> <div class="sub-box"> <h4>S'ASSEOIR (Past participle: <em>assis</em>)</h4> <ul class="conj-example-list"> <li>Je <strong>me suis</strong> assis(e)</li> <li>Tu <strong>t'es</strong> assis(e)</li> <li>Il <strong>s'est</strong> assis / Elle <strong>s'est</strong> assise</li> <li>Nous <strong>nous sommes</strong> assis(es)</li> <li>Vous <strong>vous êtes</strong> assis(e)(s)</li> <li>Ils <strong>se sont</strong> assis / Elles <strong>se sont</strong> assises</li> </ul> </div> <div class="sub-box"> <h4>🥪 The Negation Sandwich (Ne ... pas)</h4> <p>In a negative sentence, wrap <strong>ne</strong> and <strong>pas</strong> around the <em>reflexive pronoun + auxiliary</em> together. The past participle stays outside!</p> <div class="formula-banner small"> Subject + <strong>ne</strong> + [Pronoun + Auxiliary] + <strong>pas</strong> + Past Participle </div> <p class="example-mini">✅ <em>Je <strong>ne</strong> me suis <strong>pas</strong> assis.</em></p> <p class="example-mini">✅ <em>Ils <strong>ne</strong> se sont <strong>pas</strong> tus.</em></p> </div> </div> </article> <!-- Lesson 3: Agreements --> <article class="lesson-card"> <div class="lesson-badge">Recipe 3</div> <h3>Gender &amp; Plural Agreements (Les Accords)</h3> <p>When do you add extra letters to the end of the past participle? Check your auxiliary verb first!</p> <div class="two-col"> <div class="sub-box border-etre"> <h4>✅ WITH ÊTRE (Including Reflexives): AGREE!</h4> <p>Match the past participle to the <strong>gender and number of the subject</strong>:</p> <table class="mini-table"> <thead> <tr><th>Subject Type</th><th>Ending</th><th>Aller</th><th>S'asseoir</th></tr> </thead> <tbody> <tr><td>Masculine Singular (<em>Il</em>)</td><td><strong>nothing</strong></td><td>allé</td><td>assis</td></tr> <tr><td>Feminine Singular (<em>Elle</em>)</td><td><strong>+ e</strong></td><td>allé<strong>e</strong></td><td>assis<strong>e</strong></td></tr> <tr><td>Masculine Plural (<em>Ils</em>)</td><td><strong>+ s</strong></td><td>allé<strong>s</strong></td><td>assis*</td></tr> <tr><td>Feminine Plural (<em>Elles</em>)</td><td><strong>+ es</strong></td><td>allé<strong>es</strong></td><td>assis<strong>es</strong></td></tr> </tbody> </table> <p class="note-text">*Trap alert! Because <em>assis</em> already ends in <strong>-s</strong>, do NOT add another <em>s</em> for masculine plural (<em>Ils se sont assis</em>).</p> </div> <div class="sub-box border-avoir"> <h4>🚫 WITH AVOIR: NO SUBJECT AGREEMENT!</h4> <p>In Grade 9 passé composé, verbs conjugated with <strong>avoir</strong> do <strong>NOT</strong> agree with the subject—even if the subject is feminine or plural.</p> <ul class="conj-example-list"> <li>Il a mang<strong>é</strong>.</li> <li>Elle a mang<strong>é</strong>. <em>(NOT mangée!)</em></li> <li>Ils ont fin<strong>i</strong> / pris / vu.</li> <li>Elles ont achet<strong>é</strong> des livres. <em>(NOT achetées!)</em></li> </ul> <details class="bonus-details"> <summary>🌟 Curious Bonus Rule (Grade 10+ Preview)</summary> <p><em>Avoir</em> verbs only ever agree if a <strong>direct object</strong> comes <em>before</em> the verb (e.g., <em>La pomme que j'ai mangé<strong>e</strong></em>). For regular Subject + Verb + Object sentences, never add endings with <em>avoir</em>!</p> </details> </div> </div> </article> </section> <!-- TAB 3: LES 50 VERBES --> <section id="tab-verbes"
