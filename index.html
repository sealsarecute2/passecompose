Leçons : Le Guide du Passé Composé

Everything you need for Grade 9 French, broken down into three simple recipes.

Recipe 1

The Two-Part Formula & Auxiliary Verbs (Avoir vs. Être)

In French, the passé composé describes completed past actions (e.g., I ate, she went, we saw). Unlike English, it always needs two parts:

Subject + Auxiliary Verb (Present of Avoir or Être) + Past Participle

1. Present Tense of AVOIR (42 of your 50 verbs)

J'ai (I have)Nous avons (We have)
Tu as (You have)Vous avez (You have)
Il / Elle / On aIls / Elles ont

Note: Je becomes J' before a vowel: J'ai mangé, J'ai eu.

2. Present Tense of ÊTRE (6 Vandertramp + 2 Reflexive)

Je suis (I am)Nous sommes (We are)
Tu es (You are)Vous êtes (You are)
Il / Elle / On estIls / Elles sont

Your 6 non-reflexive Être verbs: aller, partir, sortir, venir, mourir, devenir.

Note on Impersonal Verbs (Il only): On your list, pleuvoir (to rain) and falloir (to be necessary / have to) are impersonal. They only ever conjugate with il: Il a plu (It rained) and Il a fallu (It was necessary).
Recipe 2

Reflexive Verbs (Les Verbes Pronominaux)

Reflexive verbs start with se or s' in the infinitive. On your 50-verb sheet, there are two: s'asseoir (to sit down) and se taire* (to be quiet).

  • 100% Être Rule: Every reflexive verb uses être as its auxiliary verb in the passé composé.
  • Reflexive Pronouns: Place the matching reflexive pronoun right before être (watch for t' and s' before vowels!):

S'ASSEOIR (Past participle: assis)

  • Je me suis assis(e)
  • Tu t'es assis(e)
  • Il s'est assis / Elle s'est assise
  • Nous nous sommes assis(es)
  • Vous vous êtes assis(e)(s)
  • Ils se sont assis / Elles se sont assises

The Negation Structure (Ne ... pas)

In a negative sentence, wrap ne and pas around the reflexive pronoun + auxiliary together. The past participle stays outside!

Subject + ne + [Pronoun + Auxiliary] + pas + Past Participle

✅ Je ne me suis pas assis.

✅ Ils ne se sont pas tus.

Recipe 3

Gender & Plural Agreements (Les Accords)

When do you add extra letters to the end of the past participle? Check your auxiliary verb first!

With Être & Reflexive Verbs: Subject Agreement

Match the past participle to the gender and number of the subject:

Subject TypeEndingAllerS'asseoir
Masculine Singular (Il)nothingalléassis
Feminine Singular (Elle)+ ealléeassise
Masculine Plural (Ils)+ sallésassis*
Feminine Plural (Elles)+ esalléesassises

*Trap alert! Because assis already ends in -s, do NOT add another s for masculine plural (Ils se sont assis).

With Avoir: No Subject Agreement

In Grade 9 passé composé, verbs conjugated with avoir do NOT agree with the subject—even if the subject is feminine or plural.

  • Il a mangé.
  • Elle a mangé. (NOT mangée!)
  • Ils ont fini / pris / vu.
  • Elles ont acheté des livres. (NOT achetées!)
Grammar Note: Preceding Direct Object (Grade 10+ Preview)

Avoir verbs only ever agree if a direct object comes before the verb (e.g., La pomme que j'ai mangée). For regular Subject + Verb + Object sentences, never add endings with avoir!
